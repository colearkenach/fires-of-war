# Fires of War

This is the Foundry VTT system code for playing Fires of War. The internal system ID remains `fires-of-war` for compatibility with existing worlds and compendiums.

Set an item's **Uses** to **0/0** for infinite uses, displayed as **∞** on the character sheet, action bars, Battle Forecast and Convoy. These weapons retain their full damage and properties, support Combat Arts, and never spend durability; staves, consumables, keys and promotion items also remain reusable. **0 with a positive maximum** still means broken or depleted. The editable Uses fields retain 0/0.

Hover an owned character token to see its portrait, name, HP, movement and attack reach. Moving the pointer away hides the hover forecast. **Click the token, choose Move, then click a blue square to move along the cyan route**; Escape or right-click cancels planning. Normal dragging and keyboard movement are also restricted to legal movement. The forecast disappears after movement or cancellation, even if the token stays selected.

**Movement limits apply only in a started encounter, to its participants.** Outside an encounter (or before it starts) units can move any distance: walls, solid Event Tiles, impassable terrain (including Wall and Pit), other allegiances' units and scene bounds still block them, but Move, terrain costs and Ice do not. No range tiles are drawn while exploring; Move shows only the cyan route, which detours around barriers.

Blue tiles use **Base Movement**, terrain, Foundry's scene grid movement rules, walls, scene boundaries, solid Event Tiles, and other units. Units of the same allegiance can be crossed but cannot be destinations; other allegiances block movement, including hidden units. The encounter's explicit allegiance assignments take precedence over disposition. The cyan arrow follows Foundry's intermediate movement squares, including explicit drag waypoints. During tactical movement, the native ruler's line and waypoint dots are hidden; its square highlights and distance labels remain. Native ruler labels show Foundry's measurement; the blue forecast and final validation additionally include this system's terrain costs. Final movement commits are validated independently of the visible overlay. In started combats, Foundry's token movement history counts against the unit's movement allowance and resets for all units when their allegiance phase starts. GM configuration/paste/undo and GM API repositioning remain available for scene management.

Red tiles combine the equipped weapon's range from every legal destination. Weapon ranges support single distances, intervals and comma-separated distances (for example `1`, `1-2`, `1, 3-5`). Unequipped, broken and staff weapons show no attack tiles. Attack reach is geometric and does not check line of sight. Hex/gridless scenes show the character panel on hover only; terrain movement enforcement uses square grids. The client setting **Fires of War Movement Forecast** controls visuals; the separate world setting **Enforce Character Movement** controls movement restrictions. Reload each Foundry client after updating the system.

Create a Region using Foundry's Regions tool, open its configuration, and choose **Fires of War Terrain → Terrain** on the **Identity** tab. Click **Update Region** to save. All 15 terrain types from the rules are available, plus **Wall** (impassable to every unit, including Flying) and **Pit** (Flying units only); ordinary Regions can keep **No terrain**. Unpainted map squares use Plain / Flat Ground. Higher **Overlap priority** wins where terrain Regions overlap; equal priorities use a stable Region ID tie-break. Region containment respects shapes, holes, and elevation. Movement tests terrain at grid-square centers, while combat bonuses use the token's center. Hover a square to see its terrain name, DEF/AVO, and effects in a panel at the bottom left; the selected token supplies the elevation.

Movement reductions are extra movement cost per square entered. Flying ignores movement costs, blocked terrain, and Ice's straight-line rule, but still receives the terrain's combat and HP effects. Skills named **Forest Stride**, **Mountain Stride**, and **Water Stride** grant their terrain exceptions; the Token configuration also has explicit Stride checkboxes. Ice grants two free ice squares per movement allowance, with the incoming direction preserved while on ice, including the exit. Combat movement history preserves both the used Ice bonus and incoming direction across multiple moves. Terrain Avoid affects weapon, Combat Art, spell, battalion, and offensive staff hit calculations; Defense affects physical damage calculations. These are contextual token bonuses, so moving one linked token never permanently changes its actor or other copies. Fort and Throne/Gate heal 5 HP at phase start; Swamp and Lava/Acid deal 5 damage, never reducing a living unit below 1 HP or reviving a fallen unit.

The Encounter tracker uses **Player → Neutral → Enemy → Other** phases, matching Friendly, Neutral, and Hostile dispositions for the first three by default. Click **Objectives** to configure the encounter's objective, reorder phases, set each allegiance's matching disposition, add custom allegiances, or explicitly assign units. When several allegiances share a disposition, automatic assignment uses the first match; explicit unit assignment selects Other or a custom faction. Individual Combatant configuration also has an Allegiance selector. Each combatant's initiative is set to its phase position (Player 1, Neutral 2, Enemy 3, Other 4, then custom allegiances in order), so trackers and modules never offer a roll. Units within a phase can act in any order and finish with **Wait / ✓** in the tracker. The GM's next/previous turn arrows advance whole phases. Empty and defeated phases are skipped. All allied movement histories and acted marks reset at phase start; finite status durations decrement for each unit at phase end.

**Rout the Enemy** counts all encounter combatants assigned to Enemy or carrying Hostile disposition, including custom factions. Victory requires their HP to reach 0; a manual defeated marker alone does not satisfy the objective. **Defeat the Boss(es)** lets the GM select one or more encounter units, all of which must reach 0 HP. Missing or deleted selected bosses do not silently satisfy the objective. **Defend / Survive** counts complete rounds from the round where it was configured (round 1 before combat starts): six full rounds from round 1 complete on entering round 7. Rewind adjusts the countdown. When a phase begins, every client viewing that encounter sees a **"Player Phase" / "Enemy Phase"** banner (Neutral, Other and custom allegiances too). Each client can turn the banner off (**Phase Announcements**) and set **Phase Sound Volume**; the GM picks a sound for the Player, Neutral, Enemy and Other phases in Configure Settings (custom allegiances use the Other sound). Victory is announced once by the active GM, displayed in the tracker, and stops forward phase navigation while preserving the encounter for review; edit/clear the objective to continue, or use Foundry's End Combat button to close it. **Seize** and **Escape** use the Event Tiles described below. Encounter settings are saved per encounter. A macro can open setup with `game.firesOfWar.openEncounterSetup()`.

Select an owned character token to show **Inventory**, **Combat Arts**, **Skills**, and **Spells** in place of Foundry's macro hotbar. The six stats above them come directly from the Equipment page: Damage, Attack Speed, Base Hit Rate, Crit Rate, Avoid, and Dodge, including skill effects that already apply. Base Hit Rate shows the Equipment hit rate (including the equipped weapon), before target Avoid and attack-specific modifiers. Hover a stat for its breakdown. Deselect the character to hide the action bars; when several tokens are selected, the most recently selected character supplies the actions. Each client can disable **Character Action Bars** in system settings to restore Foundry's macro hotbar.

Terrain DEF and Avoid modifiers are included in the unit's prepared attributes and combat stats, so the character sheet and action bars show the temporary totals. A terrain notice appears on the Personal Data and Equipment pages. Entering/leaving terrain or editing/deleting a Region refreshes these totals automatically; saved stats, growths, and level-up caps are unchanged. Unlinked tokens keep independent totals. For linked copies of the same actor, the actor sheet uses the single selected copy, or the only copy on the current scene; when the context is ambiguous it shows base values. Attacks and action bars resolve the actual token's terrain and count the modifier once.

Every weapon and equippable item has an explicit **Equip** or **Unequip** button beneath its icon. Click an unequipped icon or its Equip button to confirm equipping it, replacing the current equipment in that category. Click an equipped icon to use it; its Unequip button removes it without using it. Inventory consumables use the same effects and remaining uses as the sheet; recognized items such as vulneraries heal automatically, while unrecognized effect text is posted to chat. Equipped accessories use their effect without spending consumable uses. Passive Skills and automated Passive (Activated) Skills open their details. Recognized activated skills roll and apply their effects automatically during combat; their icons do not need to be pressed. Active Skills run their mechanical handler when available; unsupported effects remain descriptive in chat. Right-click an action for item details. Long bars scroll horizontally, and smaller viewports arrange the four bars in two columns.

HUD weapon attacks, offensive spells, and Combat Arts require exactly one other visible targeted token in range. Combat Arts require a compatible equipped physical weapon. Staff and White Magic dialogs list visible character tokens in range, preserving unlinked token actors. Range is geometric: square scenes use the same orthogonal distance as the attack forecast, with large token footprints measured from their closest edges; other grids use Foundry's distance measurement. Line of sight is not checked. Combat Arts and spells retain their existing immediate actions.

Standard weapon attacks open a **Battle Forecast**, with the attacker on the left and defender on the right. Each side shows its token image, equipped weapon and durability, HP, damage per strike, Hit, Crit, and Attack Speed. Attack order is calculated automatically: **5 or more AS** above the opponent grants a follow-up; an equipped weapon that can reach the attacker permits retaliation before the attacker's follow-up. A faster defender receives a second retaliation. Enable **Brave** in the weapon's properties to make two opening strikes, or four with a speed follow-up (two before and two after retaliation). Brave applies to the initiator's attacks, and broken/non-proficient weapons lose this special property along with their other special abilities. Broken weapons retain the system's existing penalized attacks. The **Weapon Triangle is automatic**: Sword › Axe › Lance › Sword, Bow › Firearm › Knife › Bow and Anima › Light › Dark › Anima (+15 Hit/+3 Mt, or −15/−3), never across groups; unarmed, monster, staff and stone weapons are outside it. Reverse inverts the matchup for both wielders, Superior applies its bonus only against the same weapon type, Abuse doubles it, Beyond Morality cancels it, and broken or non-proficient weapons keep the triangle but lose Reverse/Superior. A ▲/▼ badge beside each weapon shows the result. Combat Arts use the triangle too. The world setting **Weapon Triangle Override** (off by default) restores a manual selector (Automatic / None / Advantage / Disadvantage) in the forecast. Terrain uses each actual token, including linked copies.

Choose **Attack** to resolve strikes one at a time in forecast order. Successful skill activations appear above their unit before **HIT** or **CRIT** appears above the receiving token, followed by a fading damage text box. Misses display **MISS** without skill or damage popups. Each strike finishes before retaliation or a follow-up begins; all clients viewing the scene see the sequence for visible tokens. Chat retains a result for each completed strike, with successful skills included instead of separate automatic activation messages. **Apply Combat Damage Automatically** is enabled by default: damage updates HP and a fallen unit immediately stops attacking or retaliating. At 0 HP, Enemy-allegiance or Hostile tokens receive Foundry's **Dead** status, are marked defeated in the encounter, and become hidden after their damage popup fades. Other HP changes also mark fallen enemies Dead and hidden. Friendly and neutral units remain visible. Disable automatic damage to retain manual HP adjustment; direct weapon/status effects still resolve, while kill-based skills require automatic damage. Attacks and other unit actions are resolved by the active GM when available, with ownership, range and action state revalidated. All participating clients, including the GM, should reload after this update. An active GM is required for actions that update another owner's actor. Sheet attacks with no target still support untargeted rolls; retaliation range requires identifiable tokens for both units on the map.

Reload each Foundry client to load the replacement hotbar. Run the Node test suite with `FEUE_FOUNDRY_APP` pointing to your installed Foundry `resources/app` directory to include the native grid integration tests. Verify HUD behavior in a running Foundry player session.

Configure a Tile's **Event Tile** section on its Position tab: **Interactable**, **Solid**, an event name, and **Activate / Open Door / Open Chest / Seize / Escape / Break / Reinforcements**. Solid Tiles block every square their rectangle overlaps, including rotated Tiles, even for Flying units. Stand orthogonally adjacent to a solid Tile or on a non-solid Tile. Choose **Interact** above the selected unit's action bars to see nearby actions. Square grids are required. Player requests are revalidated and resolved by the active GM, who must also reload after installing the update.

Token configuration has **Interaction** below **Terrain Strides**. **Interact**, **Talk**, and **Can be rescued** default to enabled, including existing tokens. Disabling Interact disables that unit's interactions and targeting for interactions. Talk requires an adjacent unit of any allegiance. Rescue requires an adjacent ally of the same allegiance or a neutral unit, and the rescuer's **Aid must be strictly greater than the target's Build**. A carrier cannot be rescued or carry a second unit. The carried token is hidden in the rescuer's square, follows movement and elevation, and cannot move independently. **Drop** highlights the free adjacent squares on the map (respecting occupied squares, solid Tiles, terrain, walls and scene bounds): click one to set the unit down, or right-click/Esc to cancel. Choosing **Rescue / Drop** while carrying goes straight to the square picker. A carrier can also **Trade** with the unit it carries. Interact also lists **Trade** for each adjacent ally. Deleting a carrier or target cleans up the corresponding Rescue links.

The **Rescue** checkbox on the character sheet's Personal Data page applies the carrying penalty: current SPD and Max SPD are halved, rounded down. Base and Current Movement are also halved unless the unit is Mounted or Flying. Attack Speed and Avoid use the reduced SPD. Penalties affect prepared values only; permanent stats, growths, and level-up caps are preserved. Rescue interactions turn the checkbox on automatically; Drop turns it off. Clearing it while carrying releases the unit into the first legal adjacent square, or keeps Rescue active if no square is available. With no carried token, the checkbox can also be used to manage the penalty manually.

Open requires an inventory item named **Door Key** for doors, **Chest Key** for chests, **Lockpick** for either (only with the **Lockpick Usage** skill), or a skill named **Locktouch** (case-insensitive). Locktouch is free; otherwise Open spends one remaining item use, or one quantity for an item without a use pool. Opening marks the Tile Opened and clears Solid. Optionally link a native door wall to open it too. To reset an opened object, clear Opened and restore Solid as appropriate; close its linked wall separately. Talk and Activate post to chat, and Activate records its activated state and may be repeated.

**Chest rewards:** choose **Open Chest** in Event Tile configuration and select a world weapon/item, or drag one from a compendium or character inventory onto **Chest reward**. The Tile stores its own copy, including quantity and remaining uses, so the original can later change or disappear. Opening awards the copy unequipped. Characters have five weapon/item slots; a full inventory sends the reward to the opener's Party Convoy, or the Party chosen in **Overflow Convoy**. Membership uses the base character even for an unlinked token; the token receives inventory rewards in its own inventory. If no Party can be found, or multiple Parties match without an override, the chest stays closed and the key is not spent. Rewards are granted once per opening; resetting Opened lets the chest give its attached reward again. Automatic overflow works during combat without a Convoy unit. A GM event macro can still supply additional contents or dialogue; its context includes `reward` with the awarded `item`, `recipient`, and `inConvoy`.

**Convoy:** the Party Sheet stores weapons and inventory items as embedded Items, with no Convoy slot limit. Select an owned Party member, then **Deposit from Unit** or **Withdraw** to move an item, preserving quantity and remaining uses. Deposited and withdrawn equipment is unequipped. Drag carried items onto Convoy to deposit, or drag Convoy items onto a member's character sheet to withdraw. World/compendium drops and **Add Item** add copies; Party owners and GMs can also discard stock. Member owners can transfer their own items even without Party ownership. An active GM resolves player transfers. Full recipient inventories and failed transfers preserve the stored item.

**Convoy in combat:** while any member of the Party is in a started encounter, players reach the Convoy through **Interact**. Tick **Convoy** under Movement on a character's sheet to make it a Convoy unit. A Convoy unit, and any Party member standing beside it (an adjacent ally, or the unit it carries), sees **Convoy — Party name** in its Interact list. That opens a window where the unit can store and take any number of items, watching its five slots; **Confirm transfers** resolves them together on the active GM and spends that unit's Major Action (nothing is spent if the window is cancelled). The Convoy unit's own action is not used. Disabling Interact on a Convoy unit's token stops it serving adjacent units. Carried or escaped units cannot reach the Convoy. During combat the Party sheet's Convoy panel, character-sheet drops and normal Party Item edits are limited to the GM, who can still add, discard or move stock; the panel lists the Party's Convoy units. Outside combat, owned Party members can freely deposit/withdraw from the Party sheet, and Interact → Convoy also works without any action cost.

**Seize:** place interactable Seize Tiles and choose Seize in the encounter's **Objectives**. Set the objective allegiance (Player by default). Units of that allegiance use Interact to secure each Tile; victory requires all of them. The tracker displays secured/required counts. New Seize Tiles join the objective, while deleting an originally required Tile does not count as securing it. Starting the encounter clears Seize progress.

**Escape:** place interactable, non-solid Escape Tiles and choose Escape in **Objectives**. Require **all units of the objective allegiance** or **one selected encounter unit**. Any exit works; reaching or walking across an exit automatically escapes eligible units at the first exit reached, even with their Interact toggle off. A rescued passenger escapes with the carrier. Escaped tokens are hidden, stop blocking squares or taking phases, and are marked Escaped in the tracker. Their visibility is restored when the encounter is deleted or restarted. Slain or deleted required units do not satisfy Escape. Reinforcements belonging to the objective allegiance also have to escape in all-units mode.

**Break:** set a destructible Tile's **DEF**, **Current HP**, and **Maximum HP**. Choosing Break initially enables Solid. Interact strikes once with the equipped, unbroken attacking weapon, uses its normal attack power against the object's DEF (including magical weapons), guarantees 100% hit and 0% crit, and spends one weapon use when it has a durability pool. Damage cannot go below 0. At 0 HP the Tile becomes non-solid and cannot be struck again. An optional linked native wall loses movement, sight, light, and sound blocking when destroyed. To repair, restore HP and Solid; restore the linked wall's blocking separately.

**Reinforcements:** choose a non-solid Reinforcements Tile, an existing world character Actor as the **Unit type**, optional allegiance, and **Units per wave**. Enable the schedule using either **Specific rounds** (for example `3, 5`) or **Every X rounds** with a **First round**. Turns here mean encounter rounds; waves arrive at the start of the round and are resolved by the active GM. A unit occupying any part of the Tile skips that wave. The first spawn uses the Tile's center square; extra spawns use free orthogonally adjacent spaces, respecting walls, solid Tiles, scene bounds, and the chosen unit's impassable terrain restrictions, without applying its movement budget. Units that lack a legal space are skipped. Each spawned token is unlinked with full HP, no Rescue penalty, and joins the encounter. A saved wave record prevents duplicate spawns after reload or rewind; skipped waves are not retried during the same round. Starting a fresh encounter resets schedules. Reinforcement Tiles do not need Interactable enabled.

The GM can choose an **Event macro** on a Tile or unit. It runs after Talk, Open, Activate, Seize, Escape, or Break and receives `action`, `actor`, `token`, `source` (TokenDocument), `target` (TokenDocument or TileDocument), `scene`, and the requesting `user`; Break also supplies `strike` with damage and remaining HP. Only GM-configured macros execute with GM authority. All interactions also emit `Hooks.callAll("feueInteraction", context)`. Macros can display dialogue, award chest contents, or operate buttons and other map objects. The macro shortcut `game.firesOfWar.openInteractionMenu(token)` opens the interaction menu.

## Weapon qualities, skills and unit commands (13.0.11)

Select an owned unit to highlight **Move, Trade, Rescue / Drop, Interact, Dismount / Mount, and Wait** in the bottom command bar. During an encounter, three gems at the right of that row show what the unit has left this phase: **Major** (red), **Move** (blue, with the squares left) and **Free** (green). A gem is lit while that action is available and dims once it is used; hover a gem for details. All gems are dim outside the unit's own phase. Left-click selection does not open a Commands popup. Trade and Rescue appear when allies are adjacent; Drop remains accessible while carrying a unit. Available commands are highlighted above the action bars; unavailable commands are dimmed. Trade gives or exchanges inventory documents, preserving remaining uses and quantity and unequipping transferred equipment. Five inventory slots are enforced. Giving to an ally is allowed; taking its items requires owning that ally or using the GM account. Vehicle weapons cannot be traded, transferred through Convoy, or awarded as carried chest items.

In an encounter, each unit can take one Major Action in its allegiance phase. Attacking, using a Combat Art, casting, using a staff/item, activating a Major Action skill, ordering a Battalion, trading, and interacting consume it. Ordinary movement then ends. **Canto** retains only the unspent movement, including terrain costs; it never grants another attack. Mounted/Flying unit types grant innate Canto, and equipped Fetters of Dromi also grant it. **Wait** ends remaining movement and actions. The GM can reset an acted unit from the tracker. Phase start clears action state, mount-after-attack restrictions, refresh eligibility, and movement history. Outside encounters, action limits are inactive.

**Dismount** is a Free Action. The prepared unit becomes Infantry and loses Mounted, Flying and Dragon types, so terrain access, Slayer weaknesses, Aid and Rescue penalties use the dismounted profile. The stored class and mounted types are preserved, and Mount restores them. Mount is forbidden after attacking and after Wait. Dismount disables innate mounted Canto; a separately learned Canto skill and equipped Fetters of Dromi remain effective. The rulebook specifies no fixed movement reduction for Dismount; the class movement allowance is retained, with Infantry terrain and Rescue costs applied.

All listed weapon properties have controls on the Weapon tab. Unwieldy blocks speed follow-ups; Slow and Vehicle block retaliation; Reverse and Superior alter the selected triangle; Abuse doubles its modifiers. Slayer doubles damage after defense, Puncture omits the damage stat, and Piercing ignores defense. Magical and Mechanical select MAG and SPD. Brave, Deadly, Shade, Absorb and Cursed resolve mechanically. Cursed redirects damage before enemy HP/status effects or healing. Poison and Petrify apply three-turn statuses; Petrification prevents actions/retaliation and grants its defensive effects. Poison rolls its damage at phase start. Crippling adds one stacking STR/DEF penalty per damaging hit, up to its weapon-rank cap, and expires after one turn. Smash pushes into legal unoccupied squares, stopping at walls, terrain or obstacles. Vehicle attacks require Ballista Use or Cannon Use. Legacy string/array properties are recognized and retained when editing the weapon.

### Stat breakdowns

Hover (or keyboard-focus) any number on the character sheet to see where it comes from: every ability score, its cap, growth rates, max HP, Base Movement, and the Equipment page's Damage, Attack Speed, Hit Rate, Critical Rate, Avoid, Dodge and Aid. The same breakdowns appear on the six action-bar stats and on the Battle Forecast's DMG, HIT, CRIT and AS. Each tooltip lists the class base, level-up gains, items, equipped weapon, mastery, skills, timed effects (Rally, Seal, Crippling...), statuses, terrain and Rescue, and the listed terms always add up to the displayed value. A **Situational** section lists skill effects that depend on the opponent or circumstances (for example Death Blow "when initiating combat", or Blood Fury "at ≤25% HP") and whether they apply right now. Forecast tooltips split each value into the attacker's side and the target's Avoid, Dodge or DEF/RES, plus damage-taken adjustments and multipliers.

The Equipment and action-bar stats include skill effects that already apply before choosing a target: equipped-weapon bonuses (Faires, Critical [Weapon] +X, Shadowgift, Unarmed Combat), HP thresholds, the current turn and phase (Biorhythm, Quickburn, Lucky Seven, Patience, Prescience), terrain (Elbow Room, Take Cover), the map environment (Outdoor Fighter) and nearby units (Charm, Solidarity, Heartseeker, Focus, Tantivy...). Linked actors use the single selected token, or the only token on the scene. Non-proficient weapons halve the final Damage after these bonuses.

### Skill automation

Every skill in the rulebook's Skill List and Enemy Only Skill List has built-in behavior, apart from seven that need the GM's judgement (Stealth, Replicate, Summon, Dark Summon, Summon Auroras, Fell Rebirth and Trap Disarmer). Each skill on the character sheet shows a badge: **Auto**, **Partial**, **GM** or **Manual** (no built-in handler). Hover the badge, or open the skill, for a summary of what the system does. Skill names ignore case and bracket style, so compendium spellings such as "Critical (Axe) +15", "Acrobatic" and "Rally Move" match their rulebook skills.

- **Prepared bonuses** (always on, or at an HP threshold): Avoid/Dodge/Hit/Crit/stat +X, Blood Fury, Defiant Speed, Resolve, Gamble, Loptous Blood, Ultra Heavyweight, Great Haul.
- **Combat bonuses** (applied per strike and shown in the forecast): Aggressor, Quick Draw, Strong Riposte, Certain/Uncanny/Death Blow, Darting Blow, Faires, Breakers, Critical [Weapon] +X, Shadowgift, Unarmed Combat, Beastbane, Wyrmsbane, Big Game, Life and Death, Ancient Wisdom, Warding Blow, Armored Blow, Dark Charge, Biorhythm, Evenhanded, Momentum, Quickburn, Lucky Seven, Vendetta (tracked per encounter), Patience, Prescience, Keen Intuition, Alert Stance, Battle Maiden, Duelist, Focus, Tantivy, Dark Poise, Spell Harmony, Squad Tactics, Elbow Room, Natural Cover, Take Cover, Outdoorsman and Outdoor Fighter.
- **Auras**: Heartseeker, Charm, Solidarity, Demoiselle (male allies), Fierce Mien, Divine Light (Monster units), Holy Aura (vs Monsters and Beasts) and Malefic Aura (magic damage taken).
- **Activation rolls**: Luna, Pierce, Ignis, Vengeance, Dragon Fang, Sol, Astra, Armsthrift, Court Mage, Brave Attacker, Crest of Flames (Absorb, +5 Mt, no counterattack), Aegis, Pavise, Great Shield (TL difference), Miracle, Lethality, Plunder (+300 gold to the unit's Party) and Opportunity Shot.
- **On hit, kill and after combat**: Seal [Stat], Soul Rend, Poison Strike, Grisly Wound, Lifetaker, Galeforce, Bloodlust, Spinning Axe, Counter and Magic Counter.
- **Phase start**: Renewal, Better Odds, Draconic Recovery, Relief and Mantle's recovery. Poison, Burning and Blood Sacrifice deal their damage here, and effects lasting "until your next turn" expire.
- **Order, range and properties**: Vantage, Alacrity, Swift Caster, Wary Fighter, Sword & Pistol (swaps to the Sword or Pistol that can counter), Close Range/Counter, Bow/Firearm Range +X, Overdraw, Rifled Barrel, Ballistician, Axe Slam, Monster Hunter, Golembane (Slayer vs Puppet/Mechanical units), Dark Pact, Crippling Knife, Beyond Morality, Curse Proficiency and Performance Artist.
- **Damage immunity**: Mantle (only Legendary weapons and S-rank spells), Undeath (no Dark damage; Absorb hurts the attacker), Mighty King of Legend (first damaging attack each turn) and Quintessence (a second HP bar once per encounter). Magical Flight and Ultra Heavyweight ignore Slayer (Flying) and Slayer (Armored).
- **Statuses and immunities**: rulebook statuses now apply their effects. Sleep, Petrification and Knocked Out set Avoid, Dodge and AS to 0. Sleep, Petrification, Paralysis, Frozen and Knocked Out prevent actions and counters. Confusion and Guard Break prevent counters until attacked. Rattled, Burning and Buffeted apply their penalties, Shock and similar statuses reduce Move, and Silence blocks tomes, staves and spells. Divine Aura and Undeath block all statuses, and Silence Ward blocks Silence. Goddess ignores stat penalties, and Headlong Rush and Ultra Heavyweight ignore Move reductions. Adding a status from the sheet fills in its rulebook duration and shows its effect on hover. Statuses are the token status icons too (see **Conditions and token icons** below).
- **Movement and access**: Canto, Acrobat, Pass, Forest/Mountain/Water Stride, Open Field, Waterbound, Ultra Heavyweight terrain costs, Lockpick Usage (Lockpicks now require it), Locktouch, Demolish (Break destroys the structure instantly), Ballista/Cannon Use, Nobility (two Battalions), Underdog (double EXP), Weapon Training (rank-ups advance every class proficiency), Spell Mastery (half HP cost), Healtouch, Live to Serve, Terrain Resistance, Rulership (allies gain Miracle, Aegis and Pavise) and Rightful Ruler (partly automated: it triggers adjacent allies' Passive (Activated) skills at +10%).
- **Active skills** (Skills bar or sheet): Dance, Master Dance with Refresh and Special Dance riders, Rally [Stat]/Spectrum/Movement, Grand Rally, Tactical Advice, Inspiration, Hex, Pivot, Teleport, Steal, Transmute, Heavy is the Crown, Perfect Balance (once every 5 turns), Authority (extra Major Action without movement), Telekinetic Rod (opens a Battle Forecast with the equipped Staff as a 1 Mt / 65 Hit / Smash weapon), Demolish and Locktouch (open Interact). Units that move or swap places need an active GM.
- **Declared in the Battle Forecast**: Cleave (−20 Hit; each hit deals ½ damage to other enemies adjacent to the attacker), Overdraw (+1 range, +4 damage, −10 Hit/Crit; checked automatically when the target is only reachable with it) and Lunge (swap places after dealing damage).

Rulebook TNs are supplied automatically. If an item explicitly sets a non-Manual activation, its own TN settings are used instead, except Great Shield's TL-difference TN, which the item cannot express. Skill descriptions are never treated as always-on conditional bonuses, and recognized rules replace duplicate bonus fields on the item to prevent double counting. Each scene's configuration has a **Fires of War Map → Environment** setting (Outdoor by default) for Outdoor Fighter.

### Battalions

Selected units show a **Battalion** bar to the right of Inventory, Combat Arts, Skills and Spells. The bar also has an **Order** button on the sheet's Equipment page. Ordering a Battalion with an area shows that area on the map in front of its commander. The area turns to face your pointer, and the arrow keys also turn it. Click or press Enter to order it; right-click or Esc cancels. Enemies inside the area are outlined in red and allies in green. Each enemy in the area is attacked separately: Hit = Battalion Hit + CHA + LUK÷4 − the target's Avoid, Crit = Battalion Crit + CHA÷2 − Dodge, and damage = Battalion Mt + CHA − DEF. Critical hits triple damage, and Slayer types double it. Ordering is a Major Action and needs Battalion Rank equal to or higher than the Battalion's Rank.

The **Area** setting on the Battalion's sheet offers the rulebook shapes (3x1, 3x2, 3x3, 2L, 3L, 4L), *None* (affects only its commander) or **Custom**. The grid editor shows the commander square (C) facing up. Click squares to paint the area, or switch to **Place commander** and click to move the commander square, for example to leave a gap in front of it. **Extra reach** lets the area be pushed that many squares further forward while aiming, as with Three Houses gambits. Older numeric ranges still work as straight lines.

The Properties text supports `Slayer (Types)`, `Grant allies in range +2 MOV, SPD, SKL for 1 turn`, `Heal allies in area for 15 HP`, `Heal self for 10 HP`, `Swap places with an enemy on a hit` and `Magical` (targets RES). Battalion stat bonuses, such as Great Protectors' +5 DEF/RES, apply while the Battalion has Endurance, and they appear in the stat tooltips. Following the rulebook, ordering a Battalion no longer spends Endurance. Instead, a Battalion loses half the HP damage its commander takes (rounded down, minimum 1) and cannot be ordered at 0 END. Units with a Battalion cannot be rescued. Area orders made by players are resolved by the active GM, who must be viewing the scene.

Items also support **Miscellaneous / Treasure** for bullion, gems and other carried goods. These items can be traded, sold and stored, and open their details from the action bars; they cannot be equipped or consumed and grant no stat bonuses.

Combat Arts on the character sheet, action bars and token menu show only arts that meet weapon compatibility, proficiency, durability, required weapon rank and textual prerequisites. Supported prerequisites include stats (`STR 13`), weapon ranks (`Lance E Rank`, `Any C Rank`, `Sword D`, `Lance Rank E`, `any weapon at D Rank`), `Level 5` (current class level), `TL 20+` or `Total Level 5+` (Total Level), `Skill: Luna` and `Class: Mercenary`, separated by commas or “and”; “or” provides alternatives. Requirements are rechecked on execution. Rank-granted arts record their required rank. Owned arts remain stored even when filtered out.

The skill's **Activation** menu includes **Passive / Always on**. Constant stat bonuses and penalties apply without a roll. Conditional skills use the **Automation** controls: Always, own Attack, In combat (either side), Against opponent's weapon, incoming Defense, or nearby-unit Aura; an HP threshold, initiation/retaliation condition, weapon-type list, Hit/Crit/Avoid/Dodge modifiers, damage bonuses/reduction, defense and damage multipliers, healing fractions and attack-use/strike modifiers. Always-on stat bonuses use the Description tab; matching nonzero custom combat fields override Description bonuses. Passive (Activated) Attack/Defense skills roll their configured TN unless Activation is Passive. Rulebook defaults display their actual settings, and editing a field switches to Custom while preserving the rule's conditions and activation behavior.

**Pavise** halves incoming damage only for its listed weapon types after a successful activation. **Death Blow** adds +20 Crit only on attacks in combat initiated by its unit. **Heartseeker** applies −20 Avoid to adjacent enemies using the actual scene tokens and allegiance assignments. Auras update when tokens move, exclude fallen, hidden, carried or escaped sources, and do not stack copies of the same named skill. They affect combat calculations without permanently changing the actor. Effects beyond these controls can be implemented by a module or GM macro registering a rule, for example:

```js
game.firesOfWar.registerSkillRule("Blade Surge", {
    trigger: "Attack", roll: true, mult: 0.5, damage: 5
});
```

The implementation is split into `module/skills/skill-automation.mjs` (the rulebook skill table, conditions and modifiers), `module/skills/skill-names.mjs` (name matching), `module/skills/skill-context.mjs` (positions, allegiance, phase and terrain), `module/rules/status-rules.mjs` (status effects and immunities), `module/rules/conditions.mjs` (the sheet's Conditions as token status icons), `module/combat/combat-effects.mjs` (HP, healing, statuses and displacement), `module/skills/active-skills.mjs` (Active skills), `module/ui/stat-breakdown.mjs` and `module/ui/stat-display.mjs` (tooltip ledgers), and `module/units/unit-actions.mjs` (phase/action state, commands and validated GM requests). The isolated interface preview is `tests/unit-actions-preview.html`; it uses mocked units and does not modify world data.

### Capture, Subdue and Knocked Out

Tick **Capture** in the Battle Forecast when attacking an enemy (it is not offered against your own allegiance or while already carrying someone). If the combat reduces the target to 0 HP, it is **Knocked Out** instead of slain and carried in the attacker's square, exactly like a Rescue: the captor takes the Rescue penalty, the captive cannot act, and it is hidden in the captor's square. If the captive's Build is not below the captor's Aid, it is dropped automatically at the end of the captor's next phase. The captor can **Trade** with the captive (taking its items), **Drop** it on a highlighted square, or **Slay** it as a Major Action, which also drops it. Revival Stones and Forcibly Dismounted take precedence over Capture. The world setting **Capture** chooses the rule:

- **Capture** (default, the video-game rule): a dropped captive is slain.
- **Capture (dropped captives stay Knocked Out)**: the captive is left unconscious on the square.
- **Subdue**: the attack knocks the target out where it fell without carrying it.
- **Off**.

Knocked Out units stay at 0 HP (they count as defeated for Rout and Boss objectives) but are not marked Dead or hidden. Adjacent units can **Slay** a Knocked Out enemy from Interact. Healing a Knocked Out unit brings it back above 0 HP and it wakes after a turn.

### Token indicators

Character tokens show small badges: their unit type(s) at the bottom-left corner and the equipped weapon at the bottom-right, with ring colours matching the weapon triangles (Physical red, Special green, Magic purple). Dismounted units show Infantry. The GM picks the icon for each unit type and weapon type in **Configure Settings → Token Indicator Icons** (clear an entry to hide that badge; the default glyphs are in `icons/`). Each client can turn badges off with **Token Indicators**.

### Alternate rules

All alternate rules from the rulebook now have world settings (off by default), alongside the existing Holy Blood, Static Growths, Higher Stat Maximums, Fatigue, Crests, Hero's Relics, Proper Promotion and Reclassing. Casual Mode is GM discretion, not a rule.

- **Revival Stones** (boss option): give a Neutral, Enemy or Other unit up to five stones on its sheet (Player units never have them; their sheets hide the section and any stored stones are ignored). At 0 HP at the end of combat it spends one and heals to full instead of falling; spent stones return at the end of the encounter if it survives. *Enraged Revival* (per unit, more than two stones) raises max and current HP when stones are lost: 2 lost +50%, 3 lost +75%, 4–5 lost ×2. *Empowering Revival*: each skill's sheet can lock it until X or fewer stones remain, or disable it once X or fewer remain; the unit's sheet adds stat bonuses that apply at/below or above a stone count. A unit about to revive is not marked Dead, does not count as defeated, and does not trigger kill skills.
- **Modifying Durability**: *Remove Durability* makes weapons infinite (staves keep their uses), doubles weapon buy and sell prices, and makes Combat Arts cost HP. *Per-Map Durability* stops per-attack wear during an encounter; at the end of the encounter each weapon used loses 1 per-map durability (Uses ÷ 5, round down, minimum 1, i.e. 5 Uses) plus each Combat Art's cost ÷ 5 (minimum 1 unless free). A Combat Art that would break the weapon before the map ends is refused. Staves always wear normally.
- **Risky Combat Arts**: *HP Cost* pays the Combat Art's durability cost in HP; *Player's Choice* asks each time. A Combat Art cannot cost a unit's last HP.
- **Fate Points**: LUK ÷ 5 per character. Click **Refresh** on the sheet (or run `game.firesOfWar.refreshFatePoints()` for all player characters) at the start of a session. Declare **Fate Point** in the Battle Forecast to reroll the first missed attack; units can also spend one automatically to negate an attack that would kill them (on by default for player-owned units) or to reroll a failed skill activation.
- **Proper Promotion (Class Change Items)**: turns on class change items. On an item's **Item** tab (Consumable or Equippable), choose a **Class Change** effect: *Promotes the listed classes* (crests: type class names or drop class items; the unit's current class must be listed), *Promotes any class* (Master Seal), or, with Reclassing on, *Changes to an alternate class* (Second Seal). Set the item to Consumable so it has a Use button; using it is an item use (a Major Action in encounters). From Level 10 in its current class, a unit uses a crest or Master Seal to choose one of its class's promotions: promotion bonuses apply, the level resets to 1 and Total Level is unchanged. Recruit classes do not use them. At maximum level, *Full Classic* offers the promotion only with a matching item and spends it (listed crests are offered before Master Seals); *Partial Classic* promotes naturally and keeps the item. Each use spends one Use; a spent item is removed, or a stack loses one. Items marked with the earlier Promotion Item checkbox count as Master Seals.
- **Reclassing with Second Seals**: with Reclassing and Proper Promotion both on, switching class needs a Second Seal: **Reclass** on the sheet uses the unit's first Second Seal, and using a Second Seal from the inventory asks for the class. Reclassing still requires Level 10. Once a unit is in an alternate class, its starting class can be chosen again.
- **Replaceable Mounts**: mark an equippable item as a **Mount** to give it its own slot, separate from shields and accessories; its stat bonuses (e.g. +1 MOV, +2 DEF) apply only while the rider is mounted. A mounted unit reduced to 0 HP is **Forcibly Dismounted** instead: it dismounts, its max HP is halved until the end of the encounter, its HP is set to that maximum, and it cannot remount until it equips a new mount. A mount can be linked to a character Actor that becomes a separate unit beside its rider on Dismount (joining the encounter with the rider's allegiance) and must be adjacent to remount; if that unit falls, the mount is lost.

The implementation is in `module/rules/alt-rules.mjs` (settings and rule maths), `module/rules/fall-rules.mjs` (revivals, Fate Points, Forcibly Dismounted, Knocked Out and end-of-map bookkeeping), `module/units/mounts.mjs`, `module/encounter/phase-banner.mjs`, `module/map/token-indicators.mjs` and `module/map/square-picker.mjs`.

### Characters, classes and items (13.0.13)

**Conditions and token icons.** The Token HUD's status icons are now the rulebook conditions (Berserk to Knocked Out) plus Foundry's **Dead**, and they are the same list as the sheet's **Condition** section. Clicking an icon on the HUD adds the condition with its rulebook duration (or removes it); the HUD tooltip shows what it does and how many turns are left. Conditions added on the sheet, by weapons or skills, or by Capture appear on the unit's tokens, and an icon disappears when its duration runs out or the condition is removed. Edit a duration on the sheet. Units immune to a condition (Divine Aura, Undeath, Silence Ward) refuse it. Dead is listed on the sheet with an infinite duration and can be added or removed there. When the GM first loads the world after this update, existing icons for Sleep, Poison, Paralysis, Silence, Burning, Frozen and Shock become those conditions (with their rulebook durations), and icons for Foundry's other default statuses, which are no longer on the HUD, are removed. The implementation is in `module/rules/conditions.mjs`.

**Allegiance.** Foundry's Create Actor dialog has an **Allegiance** field for characters (Player, Neutral, Enemy or Other; the last choice is remembered). Each character sheet shows the same selector under Affinity; changing it updates the prototype token and every placed token of a linked character (or the token of an unlinked one).

**Choosing a class.** Adding a class to a character opens a tabbed picker (**Recruit, Standard, Advanced, Enemy Only, Monster**) listing every class in the world and in Item compendiums, with search, movement and weapons. Enemy Only and Monster classes can only be chosen for Enemy units. Double-click a class or select it and press Confirm; "Create a blank class instead" makes an empty one.

**Class sheets** now show class type, max level and movement in one row; unit types and weapon proficiencies as toggle chips with the token-indicator icons; Base (or Promotion bonus), Growth and Cap in a single table; and compact skill, roleplay-trait and promotion-tree sections. Promotions show their movement, weapons and skill count, and the promotion editor uses the same layout.

**Monster classes.** The active GM's client adds the rulebook's 20 Monster classes (Revenant to Formortiis) to the Classes compendium and the 14 Monster Weapons to the Weapons compendium, each in a "Monster" folder. Classes have the rulebook's base stats, unit types, proficiencies and innate skills, 80 HP / 30 caps, Level 30 and no growth rates (level them with the Static Growth table or set growths). Monster Weapons have no rank, never lose durability and cannot be traded or looted. Deleting a "Monster" folder makes the GM's client add that content again on the next load.

**Item icons.** Every compendium item (Battalions, Classes, Combat Arts, Consumables, Equippable Items, Skills, Spells and Weapons) uses its own icon from `icons/items/`, named after the item (for example `icons/items/weapons/iron-sword.svg`), so the compendiums look the same on every install. Weapon rims show the rank (E grey, D green, C blue, B purple, A red, S gold, Prf teal); skill tiles are blue for Passive, orange for Active and purple for Passive (Activated). New items of each type start with the matching icon in `icons/items/defaults/`.

**Stat boosters.** Items whose effect reads "Permanently increase STR by 2" (Energy Ring, Secret Book, Dragon Shield...) now raise the stat when used, through a "Stat Boosters" bonus item, up to the stat's cap; a capped stat leaves the item unused. Effects such as Pure Water's "Grants +7 RES when used, decreases by 1 each turn" apply a bonus that shrinks each turn.

**Admix.** A spell's sheet has an **Admix weapon** field. Casting the spell with that weapon equipped (or, when blank, a tome of the spell's own name) gives +2 Mt or healing, +10 Hit and +5 Crit, and costs 2 less HP (minimum 1).

**Fires of War HP bars.** Character tokens draw HP as a framed, segmented gauge coloured by allegiance (blue Player, green Neutral, red Enemy, gold Other), with a tick per HP for pools up to 40. Each client can switch back to Foundry's bars with **Fires of War HP Bars**.

**Fixes.** Multi-waypoint moves no longer stop at the first turn with "That route crosses a blocked square": Foundry v13 runs each leg separately while the token's coordinates still show the start of the animation, so legs are now measured from the saved position. Character and item sheets keep their scroll position when an edit (such as Revival Stones) re-renders them.

### Combat AI

Open the encounter's **Objectives** and tick **AI** beside an allegiance to let the computer play that allegiance's phase, for solo play or for a GM who joins in as a player and only steps in when needed. The AI runs on the active GM's client, which must be logged in and viewing the encounter's scene (square grids only); nobody else needs to do anything. It plays by the same rules as everyone: movement forecasts and terrain costs, Battle Forecasts with retaliation and follow-ups, spells and their HP cost, Combat Arts and their cost, and staves. Each AI unit moves and acts, then Waits; when the last one has finished, the phase ends by itself.

**Behaviors.** Pick the encounter's **Default behavior** under Objectives → Combat AI, and override it per unit in the **Unit Allegiances** list or in Token configuration → **Combat AI** (also on an Actor's prototype token, so Reinforcements spawned from it inherit it):

- **Charge** (default): attacks the best target it can reach this phase; otherwise advances toward the nearest enemy.
- **Hold**: moves only to attack or heal a unit it can reach this phase; otherwise stays where it is.
- **Stationary**: never moves; attacks or heals units in range of its own square, like a boss on a throne or gate.
- **Manual**: the AI skips the unit and the GM moves it. The phase ends once the GM has finished those units.

**How it chooses.** For every square a unit can reach and every enemy in range, the AI forecasts the whole exchange for each usable weapon, offensive spell and Combat Art, including terrain at the square it would attack from, auras, the Weapon Triangle, retaliation range, follow-ups and Brave strikes. It prefers likely kills, then expected damage (raw and as a share of the target's HP), weighs the expected retaliation and its chance of falling, and favors cover. Healers, low-HP units and Boss/Escape objective units are slightly preferred targets. Units heal allies with staves that have Mt and no Hit roll below 100 (status staves are left to the GM) or with White Magic spells: allies missing a quarter of their HP, or any wounded ally if the unit cannot attack. A likely kill still beats a heal. Charging units route around walls, solid Tiles and impassable terrain toward the nearest square they can attack from; enemy units slow a route rather than closing it. Units nearest the enemy act first so they do not block the units behind them, and units without attacks act last. Player and Neutral units are allies; every other allegiance fights everyone else, as for skill auras. Hidden units neither act nor are targeted. The AI does not declare Capture, Fate Points, Cleave or Lunge, and leaves Battalions, Active skills, items, Interact (doors, chests, Seize, Escape) and Rescue to the GM; set such units to Manual.

**Pacing and control.** Every client pans to each acting unit and briefly shows its Battle Forecast (client setting **Follow Combat AI**); units with nothing to do Wait without moving the camera. The world setting **Combat AI Speed** (Slow, Normal, Fast, Instant) sets the pauses; movement and combat animations always play. AI phases are marked **• AI** in the tracker. The GM's **Stop AI** halts after the current unit and **Run AI** continues (it also starts an AI phase reached with the Previous Phase arrow). Pausing the game pauses the AI, and a GM reload resumes the phase.

**Player phases.** In an encounter with an AI allegiance, players see **End … Phase** in the tracker during a phase that includes their units, and **End the other phases automatically once every unit has acted** (on by default, under Objectives → Combat AI) ends a phase once each of its units has Waited, used its action without Canto, fallen or cannot act.

The implementation is in `module/ai/ai-planner.mjs` (behaviors, exchange odds, scoring and the approach field, with no Foundry dependencies) and `module/ai/combat-ai.mjs` (planning on the map, executing moves and actions, phase control and the tracker, encounter and token controls).
